A historical reference, a proposed architecture, and a research model. Connolly’s 2012 GER case is a four-person concept; the Space 4 All v6.2 power allocations describe a six-person project. Their comparison is a scaling experiment, not a current Artemis requirement or an assessment of the original GER mission.

The core Complexity Index is intentionally uncomputed for this scenario. The earlier v0.7 demonstration score remains available on the model home page. No failure probability, TRL or validated peer rank is inferred from the new raw data.

Source and date audit

The workshop deck previously described as 2006 is dated March 2004. After LM was published in October 2019, although its NASA report number contains 2020. The engineering guide was acquired in 2022 and published in March 2023. The LPSC 2023 abstract’s reference list gives a different year for the guide; retain the NTRS publication date in citations. The 2019 nine-page AAS deck found in the follow-up search includes an internal-use marking on its last page; it is linked only in the prior chat and is not included as an authenticated public-release baseline here.

Original source attribution and copyright notices remain in the PDFs. The 2024 HuLC student project lists Connolly as adviser; it is not a Connolly-authored architecture baseline. No exact live LSSW deck has been identified.

Delivery closure

Page 19 specifies four large and six small cargo landers. At the historical nominal 8 t and 1 t capacities, 38 t is a capacity envelope only. It is not the mass of this inventory.

Per-flight payload allocation, packaging, adapters, mass margin, center of gravity and handling envelopes remain required. The 9 logistics payloads and 7 ALC may overlap contents. Phase allocation alone does not prove a flight manifest is feasible.

Generation, demand and survival energy

PUPs supply 2 kW nameplate and 100 kWh each; SEVs carry 200 kWh each. The model keeps mobile reserves separate. All availability, efficiency and outage controls are scenario assumptions.

Demand boundary

Item demand is not added to a total allocation. VECTOR is added only when enabled; confirm it is outside the allocation. An enabled but unsized VECTOR load leaves the result unknown. No lab-to-habitat scaling or biological benefit is assumed.

Supply and storage assumptions

This is a steady average energy balance, not a chronological dispatch simulation. Illumination, charging losses, temperature, aging and recovery between outages still need hardware and site models.

Construction burden changes with depth

Connolly and Carrier’s engineering guide gives an empirical density relationship in G2023, PDF page 2, equation 1. Integrating it through the excavation depth estimates the in-place regolith mass moved. This does not calculate digging force or bearing capacity.

Length, width, depth and the default 1.2 swell factor are illustrative planning choices. No measured excavation-energy factor is supplied. Energy stays unknown until you enter one. This simplified vertical-sided geometry omits slopes, rocks, ice, spoil routing and disturbance history.

Density reference curve

ρ = 1.92(z + 12.2)/(z + 18) g/cm³, with z in cm. The curve is an empirical reference derived from historical data; local south-polar material must be characterized.

Keep the nonlinear soil update separate

The LPSC 2023 abstract gives a nonlinear Winkler exponent n = 1.86 and a plate-model coefficient k = 163 Pa/mm1.86, derived by reconciling Apollo bootprints and Lunokhod cone data. The numerical coefficient depends on the stated depth units and model geometry. It is not a generic foundation spring constant. The abstract updates depth-dependent cohesion and friction behavior; do not combine its revised shear curve with the guide’s earlier linear descriptions as though they were the same model. Large-footpad loads and landing-plume behavior require their own analysis.
